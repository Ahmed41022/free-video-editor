How-to guides

Make a TikTok, Reels or Shorts video from a landscape clip

Phone apps want a tall 9:16 video. This turns a wide clip into one, and you choose which part of the picture to keep.

  1. Click Add files (or drop the video onto the window) and click the video in the list on the left.
  2. Under Presets, open the menu that says Choose a preset… and pick TikTok, Reels, Shorts. This sets the frame to 9:16, crops to fill it, and uses 1080p and high quality.
  3. A crop box appears on the video. Drag it left or right to choose what stays in the picture. Drag a corner, or scroll, to zoom in. Center the crop puts it back in the middle.
  4. Want to keep the whole picture instead? Under Frame, change Fit to Fit inside, blurred background (or Fit inside, black bars).
  5. To use only part of the clip, set the Start and End under Trim and cut, or see the trim guide.
  6. Press B for the Before / after view if you want to see exactly what the result will look like.
  7. Click Export. The video is saved next to the original as name (phone).mp4, and you can follow it in the export queue at the bottom.

Several clips at once? Set up one, click Apply to all files, then Export all. Each clip keeps its own crop and trim.

Fix a video that won't play on your phone

Game recordings and screen captures often have a frame rate that changes from moment to moment, and some phones say the video is unsupported. This makes a copy that plays.

  1. Click Add files and pick the video in the list. If its frame rate is the problem, a note under Frame rate in Video quality says so.
  2. Under Presets, choose Play on phone (keeps the whole picture). It keeps the video's shape and sound, makes it no bigger than 1080p, and gives it a steady 60 fps. Want 30 fps instead? Change Frame rate to Steady 30 fps.
  3. Click Export. The fixed copy is saved next to the original as name (phone-ready).mp4.

A whole folder at once

  1. Click Add folder and choose the folder with your recordings.
  2. Pick one video, then choose the Play on phone (keeps the whole picture) preset.
  3. Click Apply to all files, then Export all.

A video that is already slower than the rate you choose keeps its own rate, so a 30 fps video stays at 30 fps with Steady 60 fps. The original is never changed.

Trim the ends and cut out the middle

Trimming keeps one range of the video. Cutting removes parts from the middle, and you can remove as many as you like.

  1. Add the video and pick it in the list. Play it with Space, or click the timeline to move the playhead.
  2. To trim: move the playhead to where the video should start and click Use playhead next to Start (or press I). Do the same for End (or press O). You can also type a time such as 0:15 or 1:02:30, or drag the handles on the timeline. Clear the trim undoes it.
  3. To cut out a part: move the playhead to where the unwanted part begins and click Split (or press S). Split again where it ends. Put the playhead between the two splits and click Remove part (or press X). You can also right-click the timeline and choose Cut out this part.
  4. Playback now skips the cut parts. Drag the edges of a cut on the timeline to adjust it. Put back the cut parts restores everything, and Ctrl+Z undoes the last change.
  5. Click Export.

To make one video from several files, drag them into the order you want in the list and click Join into one.

Make an MP3 from a video

Pull the sound out of a video, or out of an audio file, and save it as an MP3.

  1. Click Add files and pick the video.
  2. Under Output, click Audio (MP3). (Or choose the MP3 audio preset.)
  3. Choose MP3 quality: 128 kbps, 192 kbps (the default), 256 kbps, 320 kbps, or variable bitrate (VBR (high quality) or VBR (standard)).
  4. If you only want part of it, set Start and End in Trim and cut.
  5. Click Export. The MP3 is saved next to the video. If a file with that name already exists, yours becomes name (1).mp3.

Stabilize shaky footage

Smooths out the wobble in handheld phone videos.

  1. Add the video and make sure Video (MP4) is selected under Output.
  2. In the Adjust section, change Stabilize from Off to Light or Strong. Start with Light; try Strong if the footage is very shaky.
  3. Click Export.

Stabilizing happens while exporting, in two passes, so it takes longer than a normal export. The preview does not show it, so judge the result from the exported file. The Reset the picture button in the same section puts the adjustments and look back to normal.

Speed up or slow down a video

Make a clip faster or slower, or play it backwards. The sound keeps its natural pitch.

  1. Add the video and pick it in the list.
  2. In the Speed section, click 2× for twice as fast or 0.5× for half speed, or type any value from 0.25× (four times slower) to 4× (four times faster). 1× is normal speed.
  3. Tick Play backwards to reverse the clip. This holds the whole clip in memory, so it suits short clips best.
  4. Click Export.
